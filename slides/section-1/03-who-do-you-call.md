# Slide 03 — Who Do You Call?

**Type:** Impact statement  
**Design:** Black, "WHO DO / YOU / CALL?" — middle line "YOU" in gray

---

## Key Message

There's no clear ownership. The developer is left searching — asking colleagues, reading old Slack threads, looking at half-documented wikis — because there's no single place or person responsible for these things.

---

## Talking Points

"Who do you call?"

"This is the question every developer asks on Day One. And the answer is always complicated."

"Sometimes it's the DevOps team. But they're not called DevOps anymore — they're called the platform team, or the SRE team, or the infrastructure team, or the 'cloud enablement team'. Or there is no team and it's just Steve who knows how things work."

"Sometimes you find a Confluence page that was last updated in 2021. Sometimes you ask in Slack and three different people give you three different answers and they're all technically correct for different parts of the system."

"The problem isn't that people aren't trying to help. The problem is that there is no system. There's no single source of truth. There's no self-service. There's just people and tribal knowledge."

"And that's expensive. It's slow. And it doesn't scale."

---

## Notes

- "Who do you call?" — pause here, let people fill in the gap mentally
- The Ghostbusters reference is intentional if you want to use it as a joke, but keep it light
- Emphasis: the problem is structural, not about individual capability
- Transition: "So you file a ticket."
