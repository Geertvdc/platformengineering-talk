# Slide 06 — Too Much to Hold (Cognitive Overload)

**Type:** Impact statement — core insight  
**Design:** White, "TOO MUCH / TO / HOLD." — middle line "TO" in gray

---

## Key Message

Cognitive overload is the deeper, more important driver behind platform engineering. Developers are expected to hold an impossible amount of context simultaneously — features, security, infrastructure, compliance. A platform's job is to reduce that cognitive load, not just fix the chaos.

---

## Talking Points

"I said this is about chaos. But there's something even more important underneath the chaos. And it's this:"

"Cognitive overload."

*Pause.*

"Every developer today is expected to be a full-stack engineer, a security engineer, a cloud engineer, and a compliance officer — simultaneously. They need to think about the feature they're building, the security posture of their service, the infrastructure it runs on, the cost it generates, the observability they need to add, and the compliance requirements they need to satisfy."

"And they need to hold all of that in their heads at the same time. That's not a reasonable ask. That's an architectural failure."

"The human brain has a working memory limit. We know this from cognitive science. It's called cognitive load theory. When you overload working memory, quality drops. Decisions get worse. Things get missed. Incidents happen."

"The ticket queue isn't just slow — it's a cognitive interrupt. Every time a developer has to stop and figure out who to ask about a namespace, they're spending cognitive budget they should be spending on their actual job."

"Platform engineering, done well, is not just an infrastructure problem. It's a cognitive design problem. A good platform removes the need for developers to think about the things that shouldn't require their attention."

"Build features. Let the platform handle the rest."
