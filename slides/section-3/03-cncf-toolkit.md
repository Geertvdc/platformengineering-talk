# Slide 03 — CNCF Toolkit

**Type:** Framing — ecosystem as toolbox, not a product
**Design:** Dark slide, ecosystem word-cloud watermark behind impact heading

---

## Key Message

The CNCF landscape is a toolbox, not a product. There is no "buy CNCF." Your job as a platform team is to assemble — pick a few sharp tools, integrate them well, hide the seams from your developers.

---

## Talking Points

"If you've ever opened the CNCF landscape — that giant chart with hundreds of logos on it — your first reaction is probably anxiety. 'How am I supposed to know which of these to use?' That's the wrong question."

"The CNCF landscape isn't a menu where you pick one item. It's a toolbox. Most of those tools solve one specific problem. Your job is not to use all of them. Your job is to pick a few, integrate them, and present a coherent platform on top."

"This is where teams get into trouble. They look at the landscape and try to either ignore it — 'we'll just use the cloud provider's stuff' — or they try to adopt it all — 'let's bring in every shiny CNCF project we read a blog post about.' Both approaches fail."

"A good platform team is opinionated. They pick: this is our GitOps tool. This is our policy engine. This is our secrets manager. This is our observability stack. Then they build glue, set defaults, write golden paths, and present *one* experience to developers."

"For the platform we're building today: Kubernetes for the API, Argo CD for delivery, and a handful of provisioning tools — ASO, Crossplane, KRO, Terranetes — depending on the problem. That's it. The rest of the landscape can wait."

"The CNCF gives you parts. You assemble the platform."
